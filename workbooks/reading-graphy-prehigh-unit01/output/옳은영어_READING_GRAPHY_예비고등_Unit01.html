Lesson 01

Can a Poem Cross a Border?

시 한 편은 국경을 넘을 수 있을까
Goal  번역이 원작에서 무엇을 옮기고 무엇을 잃는지 설명할 수 있다.
L①iterature carries feelings that people everywhere can share. ②Yet there is no single language that everyone in the world reads or speaks. ③Each country writes its literature in its own tongue, so no reader can reach all of it. ④Translation seems to solve the problem. ⑤But how faithful is a translation to the original? ⑥A work is born at a moment of strong feeling, when the words flow under the writer's hand. ⑦That moment never comes back. ⑧If the writer cannot repeat it, a translator in another country certainly cannot reproduce it. ⑨Literature also has a local colour. ⑩A word may sound bright and alive to people who grew up with it, and mean almost nothing to everyone else. ⑪A careful translator knows this and chooses the safest words instead. ⑫The result reads smoothly, but something has quietly been given up. ⑬Moods, images, and even thoughts are tied to words; once the words change, the rest changes as well. ⑭A translation, then, is not a copy of a book. ⑮It is a concession.

Lesson 02

This Is Not a Pipe

이것은 파이프가 아니다
Goal  이미지가 왜 사물 그 자체가 아닌지 근거를 들어 설명할 수 있다.
P①eople often say that seeing is believing. ②A photograph, we feel, cannot lie the way words can. ③Words are clearly made by people. ④They are invented sounds, written with invented letters, and joined by a grammar that a culture has agreed on. ⑤Everyone who speaks English understands the word “food,” even though each person pictures a different dish. ⑥Looser words such as “cool” change their meaning again and again. ⑦An image feels different. ⑧When we see a thing, nothing seems to stand between us and it. ⑨The picture appears to arrive directly, so we treat it as reality itself. ⑩But an image is made too. ⑪Someone chose the angle, the light, and the moment. ⑫Whether it is painted, photographed, or built on a screen, it is still a version of the thing, not the thing. ⑬The Belgian painter René Magritte turned this into a joke. ⑭He painted a pipe and wrote underneath, “This is not a pipe.” ⑮He was right: it is a picture of a pipe.

Lesson 03

In Practice an Engineer

연습할 때는 엔지니어처럼
Goal  연습과 공연이 서로 어떤 관계인지 비유를 근거로 설명할 수 있다.
C①oncert pianists spend far more of their lives practising than performing. ②Playing the piano is not only remembering notes; it is physical work that needs reflex and endurance. ③You may never forget how to ride a bicycle, but if your legs are out of shape you will not finish a race. ④So the question is not whether to practise, but how to make the long hours offstage serve the short hours onstage. ⑤My teacher used to say, “In practice a perfectionist, in performance a realist.” ⑥At home, prepare endlessly. ⑦On stage, accept what is in front of you. ⑧Do not wish that the piano were better tuned, or the hall fuller, or that you had not missed that one passage. ⑨Being a realist sounds dull when the job is to bring a great work to life. ⑩I would put it another way: in practice an engineer, in performance a pilot. ⑪The bolts of an aircraft matter enormously, but once you sit in the cockpit your eyes look ahead, not down. ⑫We practise so that we are free to fly wherever the music asks. ⑬That destination is made of imagination, not of correct notes alone. ⑭Yet without the bolts in place, we never leave the ground.
